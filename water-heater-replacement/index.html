When a failed tank cannot wait

Water coming from the shell of the tank, a bulging or rusted bottom, or a flooded closet means the steel liner has failed. The leak will not stop on its own, and it will get worse as the metal gives way. After hours, the 24/7 line is the right call, and a dispatcher can book a same-day appointment when a slot is open.

A heater with no hot water and no leak is different. That may still be a repair, so water heater repair covers how a plumber tells a failed part from a failed tank. Where the fault is the tank, the steps below apply.

Stop the damage first

  • Close the cold water supply valve at the top of the tank. If it will not turn, shut the main water valve for the house.
  • Electric: turn off the dedicated double-pole breaker. Gas: turn the control knob to OFF. A gas smell means leave, call 911, then call Oklahoma Natural Gas at 800-458-4251.
  • Open a hot tap upstairs to let pressure drop, and run a hose from the drain valve to a floor drain or outdoors only if the power is off.
  • Lift boxes, rugs and stored items out of the water. Photograph the tank, the data plate and the damage before you move things.
  • Wet drywall, insulation and flooring dry best when started early, so ask your insurer what they want before a cleanup crew begins.
  • Do not step in standing water near a panel, outlet or electric heater.

Choosing the type: tank, tankless or heat pump

You are free to change type at replacement time, and the choice affects cost, space and utility use for years. The trade-offs below are general, and your house layout makes the final call.

TypeStrengthsTrade-offs
Standard tank, gasFamiliar, fast recovery, works with a fuel line you already haveStandby heat loss, needs venting, shorter life if scale builds
Standard tank, electricNo venting or gas line, simple installSlower recovery, higher energy use per gallon
Tankless, gasEndless hot water on demand, compact, wall mountLarger gas line, vent changes, minimum flow, descaling needed
Heat pump (hybrid)Lowest running cost per gallon of the electric optionsNeeds air space, cools the room, makes condensate, higher purchase cost

A heat pump water heater pulls heat from the air around it and moves it into the tank, so it works best in a garage, basement or large utility room where cool air is not a problem. A tight closet can choke airflow, which hurts performance. Manufacturers state the minimum room volume and clearances in the manual, and the plumber should confirm them before you commit. See tankless water heater for the on-demand option in more detail.

Sizing a replacement

Tank size is read in gallons, but the number that matters is first-hour rating, which the yellow EnergyGuide label shows. It tells you how much hot water the heater can deliver in one busy hour. A household of two adults and one bathroom may be fine with a smaller tank. A family with teenagers, a soaking tub and a dishwasher running in the morning needs more.

Think about your busiest hour. List showers, laundry and dishwashing that overlap, and share it with the plumber. An undersized heater runs out, while an oversized one wastes standby heat and costs more to buy. Tankless units are sized by flow in gallons per minute and the temperature rise from your incoming water, which is colder in January than in July.

What removal and haul-away involve

A typical replacement starts with draining the old tank to a floor drain or the yard through a hose. The plumber disconnects the water lines, the gas or electric supply, and the vent, then lifts out the tank. An old 40 or 50 gallon tank is heavy when full of sediment, so stairs or tight attics can mean extra hands.

The old unit is carried out and taken away, so you are not left with a rusty tank in the garage. Ask for haul-away to be included in the written quote so there is no surprise. The plumber then checks the pan, the floor and the surrounding pipes before setting the new heater.

Expansion tanks, venting and other upgrades

Replacement is the moment to bring the setup up to date. A few items often come up.

Thermal expansion tank

If your home has a check valve, a pressure reducing valve or a backflow device, heated water has nowhere to go. Pressure climbs in the tank and pipes, and the relief valve may drip. A small expansion tank, charged to match house pressure, absorbs the swing.

Venting

Gas tanks vent through a draft hood and flue, while power-vent and direct-vent models use fans and plastic pipe. Switching type can change the vent route and wall penetration, and the manufacturer's manual states pipe size, length and clearance to combustibles.

Pan, discharge line and valves

A drain pan under a heater above a finished floor catches small leaks. The relief valve discharge pipe should run downhill to a safe point, with no cap and no reduction in size. New shut-off valves, flex connectors and dielectric unions go on the supply lines.

Planned replacement versus a failed one

Waiting for a tank to fail is a gamble. Many tanks last roughly 8 to 12 years, and a sign list helps: rust streaks at the fittings, pooling at the base, rumbling, and rusty hot water. A heater with those symptoms and a manufacture date well past ten years is likely close to the end.

A planned swap lets you pick the type, compare quotes, line up a time, and buy the unit that fits. A failed tank gives you less choice because hot water has to come back that day. Both are workable, and a swap done on the schedule you choose is cheaper in stress, even if it is not cheaper in dollars.

What decides the price of water heater replacement

A 40 gallon electric tank and a 50 gallon power-vent gas tank are different purchases, and a tankless or heat pump unit costs more again. Capacity and efficiency rating both move the total.

Moving from atmospheric to power vent, or from tank to tankless, means new vent pipe, a larger gas line or a new electrical circuit. Those add labor and materials.

A garage floor is easy. An attic reached through a hatch, a basement with steep stairs or a closet with a tight door takes more time and sometimes more people.

Expansion tank, drain pan, discharge pipe, seismic strapping where required, and the permit fee itself are separate lines. Older homes may need valve or pipe fixes to meet current practice.

Haul-away, disposal of the old tank and any water-damage mitigation are scoped separately, so ask what the quote includes.

What insurance usually does and does not cover

A policy often pays for water damage from a tank that suddenly bursts, but usually not for the heater itself when it fails from age. Slow leaks and long-term corrosion are commonly excluded. Check your policy, keep photos and the data plate, and ask your insurer what they need before repair work starts.
