First moves when the sewer backs up

  • Stop every source of water: showers, sinks, washer, dishwasher, ice maker.
  • Do not flush. One flush can add a gallon or more to a line that is already full.
  • Keep people and pets out of the room, and wear rubber gloves and boots for anything you must touch.
  • Ventilate if it is safe, and step outside if the smell is strong.
  • Do not run a vacuum, fan or electrical device in standing sewage or wet carpet.
  • Photograph the water level and damaged items before cleanup, for your insurer.
  • Locate the main cleanout and clear space around it. Leave the cap closed unless the plumber asks.

Finding your cleanout and understanding floor drains

The main cleanout is a capped pipe, usually 3 or 4 inches wide, that gives a cable or camera direct access to the sewer line. Common spots are outside near the foundation on the side where the line leaves the house, in a basement, in a garage floor or in a crawlspace. The cap often has a square nut or a round plug.

A floor drain is the lowest opening in the system, which is why it fills first. If a basement or garage floor drain overflows during a backup, the main line is almost certainly blocked. Floor drains also have traps, and one that dries out can let sewer gas into the room. Pouring a bucket of water into a seldom-used drain keeps the trap sealed.

Roots, grease or paper: what usually stops a main line

The three common culprits behave differently, and that changes the plan.

Roots enter at joints and grow into a mat. They tend to cause repeated backups on a slow schedule, often worse after wet spells when roots are active. A cutting head on a large cable can open the line, but unless the joint is fixed, roots return.

Grease forms a coating that narrows the pipe over time, often from kitchen waste. It is more likely in lines that serve a busy kitchen. Hydro-jetting clears it better than a cable.

Paper and wipes cause a sudden stop. Wipes sold as flushable do not break down like toilet paper, and they snag on roots or rough joints. These blockages are often found near a bend or a belly, and a cable usually clears them.

Insurance note

What to do when a sewer line backs up

  • Stop running water in the house, including the dishwasher, washer and showers.
  • Do not flush toilets, because the water has nowhere to go.
  • Keep children and pets out of the wet area and avoid contact with the water, which may carry sewage.
  • Do not use chemical drain cleaner, which can make a later cleaning harder and less safe.
  • Locate the cleanout cap if you know where it is, but leave opening it to the plumber unless you are sure what you are doing.
