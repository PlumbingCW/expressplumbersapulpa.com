When the drain or toilet backs up in town

Homes inside Kellyville connect to the Town sewer, which the Town bills along with trash service. A backup into a tub, shower, or floor drain can mean a clog in your own pipe or a problem farther down. The test is simple. If only one fixture is slow, the clog is probably local. If several fixtures gurgle or back up together, the blockage is likely in the main line, and that is an emergency. Do not keep flushing.

The plumber we send clears main lines with a cable machine or water jet and can run a camera to see the cause. Roots, grease, and a collapsed section of pipe look very different on camera. Our drain cleaning service handles the clearing, and sewer line repair covers a pipe that has cracked or sagged. If a backup keeps returning, say so on the call.

Homes of different ages and mobile homes

Kellyville has a mix of older houses, newer builds, and manufactured homes. In houses from the 1970s and 1980s, original water heaters, shutoff valves, and supply lines are often the parts that fail without warning. Many manufactured homes run supply lines through the underfloor space, where a leak can hide for weeks and skirting may leave pipes exposed to cold. The plumber checks pipe type on arrival.

Turn your main shutoff once a year. A valve that has not moved in a decade may not close during a flood. If it is stuck, tell the dispatcher so the plumber brings a replacement.

Hard freezes and the winter 2021 water outage

In February 2021 a malfunction at the Heyburn Lake treatment plant run by the water district left about 1,800 customers without water for several hours, according to local reporting. A cold snap can stop the supply at the street and freeze pipes inside your walls too.

Before a hard freeze, open cabinet doors on exterior walls and disconnect outdoor hoses. If a pipe bursts, close the main valve right away. Our burst pipe repair page explains what happens once the water is stopped.

Before we arrive: what to do in the first ten minutes

The steps below are the same ones the dispatcher walks you through. Only do what is safe. If you smell gas, leave the home and call 911 first, then your gas utility.

Shut off the main valve

Know whether your water enters at a meter near the road, in a crawlspace, or inside the house. Close the valve nearest the leak, and if you are on a rural water district meter, you can also close the valve at the meter.

Kill power to wet areas

If water is near outlets, a water heater, or a panel, switch off the breaker first, but only if the breaker box is dry and you can reach it without stepping in water.

Clear the area

Move rugs, boxes, and electronics out of the way. With a sewer backup, keep children and pets out of the room and do not use any fixtures that drain to that line.
