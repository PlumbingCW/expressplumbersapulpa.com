When a business plumbing problem is an emergency

Some problems cannot wait for business hours. Sewage coming up through a floor drain, an overflowing grease interceptor, a burst supply line or a leak above electrical equipment all justify an after-hours call. Restaurant plumbing: grease traps and kitchen drains

Grease is the common culprit in restaurant backups. Cooking fat cools in the line and builds a layer inside the pipe, narrowing it until food waste catches. A grease trap or interceptor catches fat before it enters the sewer by letting wastewater sit, so the grease floats and the clean water flows out.

Grease trap cleaning

A trap works only if it is pumped before it fills. Many kitchens clean on a regular schedule, set by trap size and how much cooking happens. Signs a trap is overdue include slow sinks, odor and grease backing into the kitchen. Ask your landlord or local authority about any required cleaning schedule, because we have not confirmed a Sapulpa grease program and will not guess at one.

If a trap overflows after hours, tell the dispatcher. A plumber can clear the line, and the trap itself is usually emptied by a licensed hauler.

Kitchen drain cleaning

When the line past the trap is clogged, the plumber uses a cable machine or a water jet. For a restaurant, a camera inspection shows whether the pipe is coated with grease or damaged. See drain cleaning and hydro-jetting for how those methods work.

Floor drains: the quiet problem

Floor drains in kitchens, restrooms and mechanical rooms catch spills and runoff, and they often sit unused for weeks. Their U-shaped trap holds water as a seal against sewer gas, and when that water evaporates, odor enters the room. A drain that smells but is not clogged may only need water poured into it or a trap primer.

When a floor drain backs up, the cause is often further down. Several drains may share a line, so if two fixtures back up together, the blockage sits in the shared branch or the main. A plumber clears it with a cable or jet and may camera the line to see why it keeps stopping.

Storm drain cleaning

Parking lots, roofs and loading docks drain through storm lines that fill with leaves, silt, trash and the occasional tennis ball. Water ponding in a lot after rain, or a downspout that spits water out of its base, suggests a blockage. A plumber can jet the line and camera it to check for crushed or offset pipe.

Storm and sanitary lines are separate systems, and the plumber needs to know which one you have. Do not pour anything into a storm drain. Ask the owner or property manager who maintains drains on shared lots, because responsibility often depends on the lease.

Backflow testing, a planned job

Backflow means water flowing the wrong way in a pipe, which can pull contaminated water back into the drinking supply. A backflow preventer is a valve assembly installed on lines such as irrigation, fire suppression and some kitchen or boiler connections. These devices are tested on a schedule so that a failed valve does not go unnoticed.

Whether you must test, how often, and who may perform the test depends on your water provider and the rules that apply to your building. We found no published Sapulpa backflow program, so call the City of Sapulpa Utility Billing or Building Inspections to ask what your property needs. Ask any tester to confirm that their certification is accepted for your water provider's reports. This is standard scheduled work, not an emergency, so book it ahead of a due date.

Commercial leak detection, honestly

Finding a leak in a commercial building is harder than in a house. Pipes run through ceilings, walls and slabs, and a stain on the ceiling may be far from the source. A plumber can check the meter with all fixtures off, pressure test sections, listen for leaks with acoustic equipment, and use thermal imaging, but none of these locates every leak in every building.

Some leaks need access that means opening a wall or ceiling. You should expect the plumber to say clearly what was found, what was ruled out, and what remains uncertain. See leak detection for how the methods work.

Insurance, landlords and tenants

Who pays depends on the lease and the policy. A commercial property policy often treats sudden water damage differently from long-term seepage or maintenance neglect, and tenants carry their own coverage for contents. Check your policy and lease, notify the landlord in writing, and document the damage before cleanup.

What to do first in a commercial plumbing emergency

  • Close the main water valve for the building or the affected tenant space, and tell staff where it is.
  • Keep customers and staff away from standing water near electrical equipment, and do not touch outlets or cords in wet areas.
  • Stop using restrooms and floor drains if sewage is backing up, and block off the area.
  • Do not pour chemical drain cleaners into a line that is backing up, because they can splash back and hurt the next person who works on it.
  • Call the line with the address, the suite number and the building contact, and say whether food service is affected.
