When a leaking or jammed disposal is an emergency

Most disposal trouble is an annoyance. Two situations are not. Water running into the sink cabinet soaks the cabinet floor and can reach the subfloor, so stop using the sink and shut off the dishwasher. A burning smell, smoke, or a unit that trips the breaker again after one reset points to an electrical fault, and that call should not wait for morning.

A plain jam is rarely urgent. The motor hums, nothing spins, and the sink drains slowly. Try the reset steps below, and if this is your only kitchen drain, ask for a same-day visit when available.

What you hear and see when a disposal is failing

  • A loud hum with no spinning means a jam or a seized motor.
  • Silence when you flip the switch often means a tripped overload or a dead outlet.
  • Grinding or rattling can be a spoon, bottle cap or bone in the chamber.
  • Water dripping into the cabinet points to one of four seams, covered below.
  • A rotten-egg smell usually comes from food film in the chamber.

How to reset a jam without putting a hand near the blades

Never reach into a disposal, even with the switch off. A wall switch can be bumped back on, so use the breaker.

  1. 1Kill the power.Flip the breaker for the kitchen circuit, or unplug the unit if it has a cord.
  2. 2Find the hex socket.Most units have a hex-shaped socket in the center of the underside. Many take a 1/4 inch key, but check your manual.
  3. 3Work the key back and forth.Turn it against the resistance until the flywheel makes a full circle.
  4. 4Remove the object with tongs.Look in with a flashlight and pull out anything visible with long pliers. Never use fingers.
  5. 5Press the red reset button.It sits on the bottom of most units. Restore power, run cold water, and test.

If the key will not turn, or the unit jams again within minutes, stop. A repeat jam usually means a worn bearing or broken impeller, and forcing it adds damage.

Four places a disposal leaks and what each one means

Dry the unit with a towel, run water for a minute, and watch for the first drip. Its location tells the plumber what is wrong before a screw comes out.

Leak locationLikely causeTypical fix
Top, at the sink flangeDried plumber's putty or loose mounting ringReseat the flange with fresh putty
Side, at the dishwasher inletLoose clamp or cracked rubber nippleTighten or replace the hose connection
Housing wall or bottomRusted shell or failed internal sealReplace the unit
Discharge, at the drainLoose gasket or worn elbowReplace the gasket and elbow

A leak through the housing cannot be patched with sealant. The metal shell has failed, and the repair is a new disposal.

Installing a new disposal

The old flange comes out, the sink opening is cleaned, and the new mounting ring is seated with plumber's putty or the manufacturer's gasket. The motor housing locks onto the ring, the discharge elbow reconnects to the trap, and wiring is finished last, either to a cord and outlet or hardwired through a switch.

The dishwasher knockout plug and the air gap

New disposals ship with a plug inside the dishwasher inlet. If a dishwasher hose connects to the disposal, that plug must be knocked out with a screwdriver and hammer, and the loose disk fished out of the chamber. Skipping this is the usual reason a dishwasher fills with dirty water after a swap. With no dishwasher, leave the plug in or the open port will leak.

An air gap is a small fitting on the sink deck that breaks the connection between the dishwasher drain and the sink drain, so dirty water cannot siphon back. Some kitchens use a high loop in the hose instead. Ask City of Sapulpa Building Inspections what applies if you are remodeling, since the plumber builds to whatever your installation requires.

What can and cannot go down the disposal

  • Fine to grind: small bits of cooked food and soft fruit and vegetable trimmings, with plenty of cold water.
  • Keep out: grease, oil and fat, which cool and coat the pipe.
  • Keep out: coffee grounds in quantity, eggshell piles and potato peels, which pack into a paste.
  • Keep out: bones, fruit pits, glass, metal, plastic and anything not food.

Run cold water for 15 to 20 seconds after you switch the unit off. Cold keeps fats solid so they travel past the trap.

What to do while you wait

  • Turn the disposal off at the wall switch, then at the breaker.
  • Do not put a hand or utensil into the drain opening.
  • Shut off the dishwasher so it stops pumping water into the cabinet.
  • Set a bucket under the leak and clear the cabinet of anything that soaks up water.
  • Close the cold supply valves under the sink if the leak is steady and you can reach them.
  • Photograph the damage and the unit's label for your records.
  • Note where the first drip appears and tell the dispatcher.
