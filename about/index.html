About Sapulpa Express Plumbing

A phone line that answers around the clock and sends a licensed plumber to the house, shop or restaurant that needs one.

Call (855) 575-2890

Sapulpa Express Plumbing is a 24/7 plumbing dispatch line based at 215 E Dewey Ave in downtown Sapulpa, Oklahoma. You call, a person answers, and the dispatcher arranges a licensed, insured plumber for your address. The site you are reading exists so that a homeowner with water on the floor can find the number and understand what happens next.

What this line does and does not do

The line takes emergency and planned plumbing calls for Sapulpa and the nearby towns of Kellyville, Mounds and Glenpool. Typical calls include burst pipes, clogged drains, failed water heaters and leaks that are hard to find.

The dispatcher does not diagnose by phone and will not tell you a repair is safe to leave if the symptoms say otherwise. What the dispatcher can do is tell you what to shut off, what to avoid touching, and what information the plumber will need when they arrive.

Some jobs are outside plumbing. Gas leaks come first to the gas utility and 911, not to a plumber. Electrical faults, roofing leaks and septic tank pumping are handled by other trades, and the dispatcher will say so rather than take a call it cannot help with.

How a call turns into a visit

The dispatcher records your address, the fixture or line involved and what you can see, then arranges a visit and, when you need it, a same-day appointment if one is available. The plumber who comes out looks at the problem and gives you a clear price before work starts.

The line is answered every hour of every day, including holidays. Planned work such as a repipe, a water heater installation or a sewer camera inspection can be booked for a time that suits you.

What to have ready before you call

Water on the floor is stressful, and a few details make the call faster. Know the address and the nearest cross street, which fixture or pipe is involved, and whether the water is clear or dirty. If you can reach the main shutoff and it turns, close it before you dial.

If you cannot find it, say so, because the dispatcher can talk you through the likely places, such as a basement wall, a crawlspace, a garage, or a box near the street. Mention anyone in the house who needs warm water or heat, such as a baby or an older relative. Those details shape how urgent the visit is.

How to check any plumber's license in Oklahoma

Plumbing in Oklahoma is licensed by the state through the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board, and its website has a license verification feature. Check any plumber you hire against it, including the ones dispatched from this line. The City of Sapulpa also requires plumbing permit work to be done by state-licensed, city-registered contractors, and says work done without a permit can be fined at least double the permit fees.

If a plumber suggests skipping a permit for a water heater swap or a pipe alteration, ask the City of Sapulpa Building Inspections office at its City Hall Annex on East Hobson Avenue what the rules are for your job.

Why the pages here are written the way they are

Each page on this site explains the problem first and the call second. The aim is that a reader who is not ready to call can still work out what is probably wrong, what is safe to try and what is not.

Prices are not listed, because a fair number depends on access, parts and the pipe behind the wall, and the plumber states the price before any work starts. The pages also say where a question belongs to someone else, such as the gas utility or the city utility office, instead of guessing.

Sources and standards

The sources behind this site are public ones: the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board for licensing, the City of Sapulpa for permits, utility billing and its annual water quality report, and model plumbing codes such as the UPC and IPC for general practice. Ask City of Sapulpa Building Inspections which edition applies and whether a permit is needed.
