When a sewer problem is an emergency

Wastewater coming up inside the house is a health hazard, and every flush or shower adds more of it to the floor.

Treat these as urgent: sewage in the lowest tub or shower, a toilet that bubbles when the washer drains, several fixtures backing up together, or sewage pooling in the yard over the line. A single slow sink is usually a branch clog and is a different job, covered under drain cleaning.

If the backup starts at night or on a weekend, call anyway. After-hours dispatch exists for exactly this.

What a failing sewer line looks like

Homeowners rarely say "my lateral has a belly." They say the house smells, the drains gurgle, or the same clog keeps coming back after it was cleared. Those are the real signals, and a few of them point at the buried pipe rather than a fixture.

  • Gurgling in a tub or toilet when another fixture drains
  • Sewer odor in the basement, garage or yard
  • A drain that clears with a snake and clogs again within weeks
  • Soft, sunken or unusually green strips of lawn between the house and the street
  • Water standing in a floor drain after laundry
  • More than one fixture backing up at once

Floor drains and the lowest shower show sewage first, because they sit nearest the line. One slow drain suggests a local clog. Several fixtures failing together suggests the main line.

How the break is found

A plumber does not guess at a buried pipe. A typical visit starts with a short interview about which fixtures back up and when. Then the plumber runs a camera on a flexible rod through a cleanout, or through a pulled toilet if there is no cleanout.

The camera shows the pipe from inside: roots entering a joint, a crack, a pipe that has sagged, a joint that has slipped, or a layer of scale in old iron. A locator receiver above ground picks up the camera head's signal, so the plumber can mark depth and position on the lawn or driveway before any digging is discussed.

Sometimes the line must be cleared before the camera can pass. That is normal. Our sewer inspection page explains what you receive from the camera work.

Spot repair, lining or replacement

Three approaches cover nearly every sewer repair. The right one depends on how much pipe is damaged, what it is made of and what sits on top of it.

Tree roots in the line

Roots do not break a sound pipe. They find a joint or hairline crack where moisture escapes, push in, and then thicken until water cannot pass. A root mass in a clay or older joint can look like a small beard on camera, and one in a long run can look like a rope.

Cutting roots with a jetter or a cutting head restores flow for a while. It does not seal the entry point. If the same joint keeps letting roots in, a spot repair or a liner over that joint is the lasting fix, and the tree's location relative to the line is worth noting for the future.

For routine root clearing, hydro jetting scours the pipe wall with water pressure once the line is open.

Cracked PVC and slipped joints

PVC sewer pipe is smooth, light and resists roots well, so a crack in it usually has an outside cause. Settling soil, a heavy load over a shallow run, a bad bedding job or a stone pressing on the pipe can all fracture it. Joints can also pull apart when the ground moves.

A cracked PVC section is a clean spot repair candidate. The plumber exposes the damaged length, cuts it out, and installs new pipe with couplings made for sewer service. The trench is then backfilled and compacted so the repair does not sag later.

Ask whether the plumber checked the grade. A repair that leaves a dip will catch solids the way the old crack did.

Bellies and offsets

A belly is a low spot where the pipe has sagged and holds standing water. Solids settle there and slowly build up. An offset is a joint that has shifted so that two pipe ends no longer line up, leaving a lip that snags paper and wipes.

On camera, a belly shows as a pool that the camera lens sits in. An offset shows as a step. Neither always demands full replacement. A short belly can be dug out and re-laid to grade, and a single offset joint can be a spot repair.

A long belly, or several in a row, changes the answer. A liner follows the shape of the old pipe, so it will not remove a sag.

Cast iron drain pipe repair

Many older houses have cast iron drain and sewer pipe, especially under slabs and in the walls and crawlspaces of homes built decades ago. Sapulpa is a historic town with homes from its early 1900s oil and glass era, so you may well find iron under an older house. We cannot say how common it is locally, so the camera and the pipe itself tell the story.

Cast iron corrodes from the inside. Scale builds up like a rough crust, narrows the bore and catches paper and grease. From the outside, the pipe can rust, flake and eventually leak at the bottom where water sits. Hammering lightly on a clean section and hearing a dull thud rather than a ring suggests thin walls.

Repair choices are narrower than with PVC.

Sleeves and couplings

A repair coupling or sleeve with a rubber gasket and stainless band can seal a short crack or a failed joint on sound iron. It works only if the pipe on both sides of the damage is solid enough to hold a clamp.

Section replacement

When iron is thin or cracked over a stretch, the plumber cuts out the bad section and joins in PVC or ABS with transition couplings made for iron to plastic. This is common under slabs and in crawlspaces.

Full replacement

If scale is heavy, walls are thin throughout or the pipe has collapsed, replacing the run is often wiser than patching it. Patching one failed joint on a pipe that is failing everywhere tends to lead to another call. Ask the plumber what the camera showed on the sections that were not repaired.

What decides the price of sewer line repair

We do not post dollar figures on this page. A real number depends on the pipe and the yard, and the plumber who sees it can give you a clear price before work starts. These are the factors that move it.

A shallow spot repair near a cleanout is a small job. A repair at the far end of a long run, or deep under a driveway, takes more digging, more backfill and more hours. Length matters most for lining and replacement.

Lawn is cheaper to restore than a concrete driveway, a patio, a mature tree's root zone or a finished room. Access through those surfaces adds demolition and rebuild work, and sometimes other trades.

PVC repairs are straightforward. Cast iron, clay or other older pipe may need transition fittings or more removal, and a worn pipe can crumble when cut, which extends the scope.

Spot repair, lining and replacement each carry different labor, equipment and materials. The plumber should lay the options out side by side with the footage and explain what each includes.

Does insurance pay for a sewer line repair?

Many homeowner's policies treat a sudden backup into the house differently from a worn or root-damaged buried line. Some pay for water damage inside, some require an added endorsement for sewer or drain backup, and many exclude wear and tear on the pipe itself. We cannot say what yours covers.

Check your policy and ask your agent before the plumber leaves, not after. Photograph the sewage, the affected floor and any damaged belongings, and keep the plumber's camera footage and written findings. If you rent, tell your landlord right away and send the same photos.

What to do while you wait

  • Stop using water: no flushing, showers, laundry or dishwasher
  • Keep children and pets out of any area with sewage on the floor
  • Do not run drain chemicals, which can splash back and harm pipe and skin
  • Move belongings off wet floors and wear gloves and boots if you must step in it
  • Photograph the backup and note when it started
  • Locate your cleanout, if you have one, so the plumber can reach it fast
  • If you smell gas or see sparks near wet wiring, leave and call 911
